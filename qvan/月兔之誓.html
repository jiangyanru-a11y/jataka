Na sasassa tilā atthi na muggā nāpi taṇḍulā, iminā agginā pakkaṁ mamaṁ bhutvā vane vasā 'ti. 这是古昔兔王对帝释天所说的话——无上布施的最后一句：不求回报，乃至不计其身。 你若能解其深意，便知真正的布施不在于所有，而在于舍离所执；此即菩萨道初起之步行。 随后，你面前浮现出一片古老的森林——四善友同在，布萨将临。你须完成三项考验，在林中亲证：无施舍之资者，亦能施一切所必要之物。 ▼

明日是布萨日（Uposatha）。兔王叮嘱水獭、豺狼和猴子：我夜观月相，明日若有乞士前来，当从自己的食物中布施，有大功德——此法乃古昔诸圣贤之传统。三友各自觅食归来，各回各自住处；你思及自己所食为草，连胡麻与绿豆也没有，如何布施于人？虽无奈，也只好回住处——每行每列各一只，且各与所宜地形相邻。

提示：灰格为地形，不可落子。空格里放齐四兽各一只；同一行或同一列不可有两只。

一、Jetavana 因缘（施一切必要物）

Satta me rohitā macchā ti.

Jātaka of the Hare — Of Charity and the Moon. “Seven rohu fish I have,” so sang the Otter—

The Master, dwelling at Jetavana, spake thus concerning the gift of all necessaries.

For in Sāvatthī, it is said, a certain householder prepared a gift of all necessaries for the Order with the Buddha at its head. He built a pavilion at his door, invited the Buddha and the monks, seated them on fair seats, gave them food of various excellent flavours, and for seven days he invited them, saying, “Come again tomorrow, come again tomorrow.” And on the seventh day, he gave all necessaries to the five hundred monks with the Buddha at their head.

Then the Master, having finished his meal, made a thanksgiving: “Householder, it is fitting that thou shouldst feel joy and gladness. For this giving of alms is the tradition of the wise men of old. They, indeed, giving up their very lives to beggars who came, gave even their own flesh.” And at the householder’s request, He told a story of the past.

二、过去世 · 林中四友与布萨

Atīte Bārāṇasiyaṁ Brahmadatte rajjaṁ kārente Bodhisatto sasa-yoniyaṁ nibbattitvā araññe vasati. Tassa pana araññassa ekato pabbata-pādo ekato nadī ekato paccantagāmako. Apare pissa tayo sahāyā ahamsu — vānaro ca sigālo ca uddo ca. Sāyaṃ te sannipatanti.

Long ago, when Brahmadatta reigned in Benares, the Bodhisatta was born as a hare and lived in the forest. That forest had on one side a mountain foot, on one side a river, and on one side a border village. And he had three friends: a monkey, a jackal, and an otter. These four wise creatures lived together, each seeking his own food in his own feeding-ground, and at evening they would meet together.

The wise hare taught the Dhamma to the other three, saying: “Alms should be given, virtue should be kept, the Uposatha day should be observed.” And they accepted his teaching.

Now one day the Bodhisatta looked up at the sky and saw the moon. Knowing that the next day was the Uposatha, he said to the others: “Tomorrow is the Uposatha. Do you three take upon yourselves the precepts and keep the fast. Alms given in virtue bring great fruit. Therefore, when a beggar comes, give from the food you would eat, and then eat yourselves.”
