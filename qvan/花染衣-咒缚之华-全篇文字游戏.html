1. Puppharattajātaka（花染衣本生）

Na idaṃ dukkhaṃ aduṃ dukkhaṃ yaṃ maṃ tudati vāyaso
yaṃ sāmā puppharattena kattikaṃ nānubhossatīti.
这是古昔那位因爱欲而夜闯国王的花园、最终被刺在矛上的男子，临死前对乌鸦所说的话。偈云：身被啄刺尚非真苦，不得与伊人共度节期，才是刺入心中的毒箭。你若能解其深意，便知爱欲如何遮蔽智慧，执著如何埋伏于微末之欢。
随后，你将被带入波罗奈城的过去世——那也是本生因缘的另一扇门。三项考验等你：咒语、花园、偈颂；步步皆是对「爱欲如毒箭」的亲历。

一、Jetavana 因缘

Jātaka No. 147: The Dyer of Flowers — Of Pains and Perfumed Robes. “This is not pain, that is not pain,” so ran the mournful cry—

The Master, dwelling at Jetavana, spake thus concerning a certain monk who had fallen into the grip of longing.

For the Blessed One said unto him: “Is it true, O monk, that thou art consumed by yearning?”

“It is true, O Lord,” replied the monk.

“And who,” asked the Master, “hath brought thee to this pass?”

“My former wife, O Lord,” he answered. “Sweet is the touch of her hand, and without her I cannot live.”

Then the Master spake: “O monk, this woman hath wrought thee naught but woe. In times past, too, because of her, thou wast impaled upon a stake. Even as thou didst breathe thy last, lamenting for her, thou didst fall into hell. Why, then, dost thou long for her again?” And so saying, He revealed the story of bygone days.

二、过去世 · 节期与贫者

Atīte Bārāṇasiyaṁ Brahmadatte rajjaṁ kārente Bodhisatto ākāsaṭṭhadevatā ahosi. Atha Bārāṇasiyaṁ kattikarattivārachaṇo sampatto hoti, nagaraṁ devanagaraṁ viya alaṃkariṃsu, sabbo jano chaṇakīḷānissito ahosi. Ekassa pana duggatamanussassa ekam eva ghanasāṭakayugaṁ ahosi, so taṁ sudhotaṁ dhovāpetvā obhañjāpetvā satavalikaṁ sahassavalikaṁ kāretvā ṭhapesi.

Long ago, when Brahmadatta reigned in Benares, the Bodhisatta dwelt as a deity in the sky.

Now in that city, the festival of the Kattika night had come. The townsfolk adorned the city as if it were a heavenly abode, and all gave themselves to merrymaking.

But there was a poor man who possessed but a single suit of thick cloth. He had it washed, folded into a hundred and a thousand pleats, and laid it by.

三、妻求番红花衣

“Good wife,” said he, “we are poor. Where should we find saffron? Put on thy plain garment and go play.”

五、捕获与处刑

The watchmen heard the sound upon the fence. “A thief!” they cried, and they surrounded him, seized him, reviled him, beat him, and bound him.

When morning broke, they brought him before the king. And the king said: “Go, impale him upon a stake.”

So they bound his arms behind his back, and as the drum of doom was beaten, they led him forth from the city and set him on the spike.

Then came great agony. Crows settled upon his head and pecked at his eyes with beaks as sharp as spear-points.

But that my dark-skinned love, in flower-dyed robe, should not with me keep Kattika’s night—that, that is pain indeed.”

七、命终

So tathārūpam pi dukkhaṃ amanasikaritvā tam eva itthiṃ anussaritvā imaṃ gāthaṃ āha. Evaṃ so tassā itthiyā anussaraṇena kālaṃ katvā niraye nibbatti.

So he, lamenting for that woman, died and was reborn in hell.

八、本生对应

Tadā yā itthī sā etarahi purāṇadutiyikā; yo puriso so etarahi bhikkhu; yā ākāsaṭṭhadevatā sā ahaṃ ahosīti.

Then the Master, having taught this truth, linked the birth-story: “The husband and wife of that time are now this monk and his former wife; and the deity who stood in the sky was I myself.”
