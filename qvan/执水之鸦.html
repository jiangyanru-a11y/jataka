3. Kākajātaka（乌鸦本生）

Api nū hanukā santā, mukhañ ca parisussati,
oramāma na pārema, pūrat' eva mahodadhīti.

这是古昔一群乌鸦在雌鸦被海浪卷走后，试图掏干大海时所发出的哀叹。佛以此喻「无益之行」：以有穷之身，欲填无穷之苦。

你若能解其深意，便知何为无益的执取，何为徒劳的爱恋。随后，你面前浮现出一片无边的海岸——那是本生因缘的入口。你须完成三项考验，在偈颂的字里行间，亲见「贪」如何叫人衔海而不自知。

此世，一群年老的比丘互相搂着脖子，泪流满面，徘徊在草棚之间。 长老甲（哽咽）： 「她的手艺……世间再无人能比啊……」 长老乙（泣不成声）： 「优婆夷……她走了……我们怎么办……」 一位比丘（疑惑）： 「诸位具寿，为何如此悲伤？出家之人，岂可仍恋世间烟火？」 长老甲： 「我们一位同修的往昔妻子日日为我们备汤备饭，如今……她病逝了。」 一位比丘： 「诸位出家人，为何还执着于旧日之缘？爱欲如毒箭，所射非皮肉，而是出离之心。」
那位年长比丘的旧日是一位富有的家主。 与几位好友听闻佛陀说法后，毅然出家: 佛陀（在法座上）： 「诸位，家如火宅，欲如毒器；三界无安，犹如火宅。若求出离，当断其根。」 一位比丘（心中默念）： 「我们老了……住在家里还有什么享受？不如出家，断苦边，求涅槃道。」 他们将财富分给子女，泪别亲人，披上袈裟。

【下一节】

然而一位比丘与几位老比丘并没有精进修行,而是在僧团之外另外建屋居住,每日托钵乞食，却总是走向旧日之家,往往从妻儿家拿了饭,又到那位优婆夷家吃饭。 直到那一天—— 长老丙（惊慌跑来）： 「她病倒了！」 赶到她家时，她已经闭目长辞。 长老相拥徘徊而泣,众比丘在法堂议论纷纷. 佛陀闻此,说起前世因缘——「尊者，此即汝昔日自取之事。」

提示一 · 元音：数韵核（音节骨节）时，以元音为准。巴利语单元音为 a ā、i ī、u ū、e、o；另有双元音如 au、ai 等。碑上范例的每一行，读来声步大体匀停；这四行里，有的行显得局促，像少踏了半步。

        
有两处细缝，各容得下一粒最小的功能语；补进去之后，意思与声气往往同时顺过来。

        
ca 与 na 应各落在第几句，须与碑上范例的声气、义理对拍。

        
对照碑上范例的读法：齐整后每一句为八个音节（八拍），且按巴利诗律可数得每句八个元音韵核。残句显得短促的那一行，往往正是少这一拍；将 ca、na 各补进一句后，四句都应满足「八音节 / 八元音」再点验证。

      


      

        
石碑发光

        
碑光入海，偈颂齐整，义译亦现。海涛深处，海神自波涛中显现。

突然，海水翻腾，一个巨大的身影从浪中升起——海神现身，面目狰狞，声如雷鸣：

        
海神：「汝之妻已逝，汝之执念，不过自苦！以有穷之喙掏无穷之海，正是愚痴所招。」

        
海神：「她已归于轮回，非汝衔水所能唤回……」

        
话头回到当下祇园精舍。佛陀说：过去世里，那丧妻而率众掏海的乌鸦，就是这位长老；死去的雌鸦，就是他出家前的妻子；从波涛中现身、喝止众鸦的海神，则是菩萨——以海神之相，破我执于愚痴之海。

      

      
      

        Jātaka No. 146 · 《本生经》巴利语节选与英译（全章回首）
        

          
通章后再读本生原文摘录与英译，或能与前文叙事豁然相应。

          
一、偈颂与 Jetavana 因缘

          
Api nū hanukā santā, mukhañ ca parisussati,
oramāma na pārema, pūrat’ eva mahodadhīti.

          

            
Jātaka No. 146: The Crows — Of Grief and Salt Water. “Are our jaws weary?” so they cried in vain—

            
The Master, dwelling at Jetavana, spake thus concerning many aged monks.

            
For in the days of their household life, these men had been wealthy householders of Sāvatthī, rich and prosperous, and they had been friends together. Hearing the Master’s teaching, they had thought: “We are old. What good is the household life to us? Let us go forth into the pleasant Buddha’s doctrine and make an end of suffering.” So they gave all their possessions to their children, left their weeping kinsfolk, and sought ordination from the Master.

          

          
二、比丘哀哭因缘

          
Atha te mahallakatherā vihāraṃ gantvā aññamaññaṃ gīvāsu gahetvā “madhurahattharasā upāsikā kālakatā” ti vihārapaccante rodantā vicariṃsu. Tesaṃ saddaṃ sutvā ito c’ ito ca bhikkhū sannipatitvā “āvuso kasmā rodathā” ti pucchiṃsu.

          

            
Then those old elders went to the monastery, and taking one another by the neck, they wept and wandered about the edge of the grove, crying: “The good lay-sister with the sweet, skilful hand is dead!”

            
The other monks, hearing their lament, gathered from all sides and asked: “Friends, why do you weep?”

            
“The former wife of our companion,” they answered, “she whose hand was so sweet in cooking, is dead. She was most helpful to us. Where shall we find such another? For this cause we weep.”

          

          
三、出家之后与说法缘起

          
Pabbajitvā ca pana pabbajjānurūpaṇ samaṇadhammaṁ na kariṃsu. Mahallakabhāvena dhammam pi na pariyāpuṇiṃsu. Gihikāle viya pabbajitakāle pi vihārapariyante paṇṇasālā kāretvā ekako va vasiṃsu. Piṇḷāya carantāpi aññattha agantvā yebhuyyena attano puttadārass’ eva gehaṁ gantvā bhuñjiṃsu. Tesu ekassa purāṇadutiyikā sabbesam pi mahallaka-therānaṁ upakārā ahosi.

          

            
But when they had gone forth, they did not live as beseemed recluses. By reason of their age, they could not master the Dhamma. Even as they had lived at home, so now they built themselves leaf-huts on the borders of the monastery and dwelt alone. And when they went for alms, they went not elsewhere, but for the most part to the house of their own wives and children, and there ate.

            
Now the former wife of one of them was a help to all these aged elders. Therefore the others also, taking the food they had received, would go and sit in her house and eat. And she gave them broth and curry as they had need.

            
But at length she fell sick and died.

            
The monks, seeing their grief, began to talk in the hall of truth: “Friends, it is for this cause that the aged elders go about weeping, taking one another by the neck.”

            
Then the Master came and asked: “What is it, monks, that ye sit here discussing now?”

            
When they told Him, He said: “Not only now, monks, do they weep and wander for her death. In times past, too, born as crows, they strove to empty the great ocean for a dead mate, and only by the aid of the wise did they save their lives.” And He revealed a story of old.

          

          
四、过去世 · 宿世为鸦（起）

          
Atīte Bārāṇasiyaṁ Brahmadatte rajjaṁ kārente Bodhisatto samudde devatā hutvā nibbatti. Ath’ eko kāko attano bhariyaṁ kākiṁ ādāya gocaraṁ pariyesamāno samuddatīraṁ agamāsi.

          

            
Long ago, when Brahmadatta reigned in Benares, the Bodhisatta was born as a deity of the sea.

            
Now a certain crow took his wife, the she-crow, and went to the seashore in search of food.

            
At that time, men had made an offering to the Nāgas on the shore with milk-rice, fish, meat, and wine, and then departed.

            
The crow saw the milk and the rest, and with his wife ate of the milk-rice, fish, and meat, and drank much wine.

          

          
五、醉浴与失偶

          
Te ubho pi surāmadamattā “samuddakīḷaṁ kīḷissāmā” ti velāyaṁ nīsīditvā nahāyituṁ ārabhiṃsu. Ath’ ekā ūmi āgantvā kākiṁ gahetvā samuddaṁ pavesesi. Tam eko maccho maṁsaṁ khāditvā ajjhohari. Kāko “bhariyā me matā” ti rodi paridevi.

          

            
And being both drunken with wine, they said: “Let us play in the sea!” So they sat down on the shore and began to bathe.

            
Then a wave came and swept the she-crow into the sea. A fish seized her and swallowed her.

            
The crow wept and lamented: “My wife is dead!”

            
Hearing his cry, many crows gathered and asked: “Why do you weep?”

            
“Your companion,” he cried, “bathing on the shore, hath been carried off by a wave!”

            
And all they, with one voice, lamented and wept.

          

          
六、衔海、偈颂与海神

          
Atha nesaṃ etad ahosi: kiṃ imassa udakassa, āvamissāma samuddaṃ suññaṃ katvā sahāyikaṃ nīharissāmā ti. Te mukhena udakaṃ gahetvā thale ussificiṃsu. Atha nesaṃ kaṇṭhā sukhārī-bhāvaṁ agamaṃsu, thalaṃ pavisitvā nisīdanti. Hanukā kilamanti, tuṇḍāni parisussanti, akkhīni lohitāni honti, niddāya abhibhūtā aññamaññaṃ evam āhaṃsu.

          
Api nū hanukā santā, mukhañ ca parisussati,
oramāma na pārema, pūrat’ eva mahodadhīti.

          

            
Then a thought came to them: “What is this seawater to us? Let us bale it out, make the sea empty, and rescue our friend!”

            
So they filled their mouths and spat the water out upon the shore. But their throats grew dry with the brine, and they would go up on the land to rest. Their jaws wearied, their beaks parched, their eyes reddened, and overcome with drowsiness, they spake one to another:

            
“Alas, friends! We take the sea-water and pour it forth, but the place we have emptied fills again. We cannot empty the great ocean.” And they uttered this verse:

            
“Are our jaws weary, and our beaks all dry?
We bale and bale—the great sea will not dry.”

            
And having so said, all those crows lamented long: “Such a beak had she! Such round eyes! Such a skin! Such a sweet voice! And she is lost to us through this thievish sea!”

            
And as they lamented, the deity of the sea showed himself in terrible form and put them to flight. Thus they were saved.

          

          
七、摄颂与判语

          
Tadā yā kāki sā etarahi purāṇadutiyikā; yo kāko so etarahi mahallakatthero; ye aññe kākā te cāti mahallakattherā; yā samuddadevatā sā ahaṁ ahosīti.

          

            
Then the Master, having taught this truth, linked the birth-story: “The she-crow of that time is the former wife of to-day; the crow is the aged elder; the other crows are the aged elders; and the sea-deity was I myself.”
